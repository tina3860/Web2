Predicting Customer Repurchase

View Code View Documentation

The Business Problem

Environmental Company, a manufacturer and reseller of vacuum truck parts, lacked outbound sales efforts and had no way to anticipate customer needs or encourage repeat purchases. Without a data-driven approach, the company had limited visibility into when customers were likely to reorder and which customers were most valuable to prioritize.

Findings

Key findings included:

Analysis of 2024 transaction data across customer, product, and time-based dimensions revealed distinct purchasing behaviors and patterns.
K-Means Clustering identified three customer segments with different spend and frequency profiles.
A Random Forest classifier achieved an AUC above 95% in predicting 30-day reorders.
Association rule mining uncovered complementary product bundles frequently purchased together.
The Cox Proportional Hazards model identified a critical repurchase window within 50 days of a customer's last order.

Results

The combined models provided a framework for segmentation-driven marketing, inventory optimization, and early lifecycle outreach. High-value customers could be identified and targeted before the 50-day repurchase window closed, increasing the likelihood of retention. Product bundle insights informed cross-sell opportunities, and the reorder predictions gave the sales team a prioritized list of accounts to contact proactively.

Impact

Environmental Company gained a data-driven foundation for launching outbound sales efforts for the first time, with clear customer segments, predicted reorder timelines, and product pairing recommendations to guide strategy.
