Product Inventory Database

View Code View Documentation

The Business Problem

Environmental Company, a manufacturer and reseller of vacuum truck parts serving municipalities, OEMs, resellers, and contractors, was operating across three poorly integrated systems — Shopify for e-commerce, Sage for accounting, and HubSpot for CRM. The lack of integration caused inconsistent product records, inaccurate inventory counts, and no way to monitor when items needed to be restocked. Rather than attempting to fix the existing systems, the decision was made to design a clean database from scratch.

Findings

Key findings included:

The existing systems created data mismatches across departments including purchasing, warehouse, sales, and leadership. Key operational questions could not be answered without manually pulling from multiple sources. After designing a relational schema built around five core entities — Product, Supplier, Category, Inventory, and Sales — SQL queries were written to answer real business reporting questions including identifying products sold after a specific date, ranking products by revenue, flagging items below reorder thresholds, and calculating revenue filtered by category and supplier location.

Results

The new database consolidated all three systems into a single unified structure with real-time inventory visibility, automated reorder flagging, and clear supplier management. Operational reporting that previously required manual effort across multiple platforms could now be answered with a single SQL query. Two primary outcomes were achieved — streamlined operations across all departments and real-time inventory visibility for warehouse and purchasing teams.

Impact

Environmental Company gained a scalable, clean database foundation that eliminated data mismatches, reduced operational inefficiencies, and improved customer service through better data accuracy and accessibility across all departments.
